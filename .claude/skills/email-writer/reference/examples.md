# Examples — the bar to match

These are the Cents sequences (originally from the Zevenue / Yusuf YouTube reference),
lightly refined into OUR voice. Structure and content are ~80% theirs, with our
mini-fixes applied (split long sentences, no "instead", no picture-painting like
"substrate", no "I talk to", no staccato fragment lists, "a comparison" not "the
comparison", no colons or em dashes). Match this structure, rhythm, and generosity.
Our own approved sequence (Stephen, at the bottom) is the primary bar.

The single biggest lesson: these do NOT explain the prospect's problem back to them.
They acknowledge a move, observe a PATTERN ("most teams in that spot end up in the
same place"), and OFFER a specific artifact. Generous, peer to peer, never selling.

## Sequence 1 — Nicholas (Sr. VP Sales)

**Signal:** $140M Series C six weeks ago. Hiring VP Marketing + VP CS. Expansion into
dry cleaners, route operators, tailors, cobblers, none addressable on Apollo.
**Rationale:** owns both VP seats, publicly named the exact problem. Lead with the
hiring gap, pivot to the non-Apollo sourcing problem.

**Email 1 — subject: vpm + 4 new verticals** (75 words)
> Nicholas, saw you're hiring both the VPM and the VP CS, and congrats on the Series C close.
> Been talking to a lot of sales leaders in that spot, expanding into new ICPs while waiting on a marketing hire. Most end up in the same place. Outbound sits in limbo for a couple quarters because no one owns the sourcing build yet.
> Happy to share what other SVPs in that gap did differently. No pitch, just a comparison.

**Email 2 — subject: re: vpm + 4 new verticals** (73 words)
> Quick add on the expansion side. The dry cleaner, route operator, tailor and cobbler side isn't addressable on Apollo. Most teams discover that two months in, after they've already promised the board a pipeline number.
> The VPM you hire will want a sourcing system already running on day one, not a six-month build to do.
> Is that already on someone's plate, or does it land on the new hire?

**Email 3 — subject: sourcing pipelines** (53 words)
> Nicholas, last one then I'll leave this alone.
> Mapped what the four expansion ICPs would source against. Google Maps + Mindbody + state license registries for laundromats, different inputs for each of the others. Five vertical-SaaS clients in, the architecture is the same and the inputs change per vertical.
> Want me to send it?

## Sequence 2 — Alex (thesis / strategic buyer)

**Signal:** Series C $140M, largest round in their history. Has publicly argued across
3 media surfaces that vertical AI works because context lives in the hardware layer.
**Rationale:** the wedge is the architectural parallel, he already made the argument,
the pitch is the application not the thesis.

**Email 1 — subject: same wall** (75 words)
> Alex, congrats on the Series C close.
> The argument you've been making lately, that horizontal AI couldn't crack laundromats because the operator context lives in your hardware, applies one layer up. Outbound into operator markets bounces for the same reason. The senders don't have the context graph the operators live in. The problem isn't the AI, it's the data it runs on.
> Curious if AI-native outbound is on the roadmap, or sitting with the VPM hire?

**Email 2 — subject: re: same wall** (60 words)
> The AlleyWatch piece gets at the broader version. Vertical SaaS reshaped restaurants, salons, gyms. Laundry is next.
> The GTM stack one layer down works the same way. The outbound architecture that ships pipeline in restaurants doesn't translate to laundromats. Different operator personas, none on Apollo, no CRM segment to lift.
> Pulled the map from five vertical-SaaS builds. Want it?

## The extracted pattern (use this)

**Email 1 structure:**
1. `[Name], [casual acknowledgment of their specific move]` (the raise, the hiring, the public argument). Congratulate where natural.
2. `Most [their type] I talk to in that spot end up in the same place. [the pattern of how it actually plays out].` A knowing, been-there observation. NEVER their problem explained back.
3. `Happy to share [specific artifact]. No pitch, just the [comparison].` or a soft question.

**Email 2 structure:** one hyper-specific piece, named concretely in punchy fragments
("State license registries for dry cleaners. Trade associations for cobblers."), then
how the good teams handle it, then a qualifying question (who owns this).

**Email 3 (breakup):** `[Name], last one then I'll leave it.` + a specific artifact you
already made (a map, a one-pager) + `Want it?` / `Want me to send it?`.

**Rhythm:** mix long setup sentences with short punchy declaratives ("Apollo for none.",
"Laundry is next.", "The wall isn't the AI, it's the substrate."). Casual, sharp, generous.

**CTA:** offer a real thing, low friction ("want it?"). NEVER a vague either/or about
their pain ("is that the bottleneck, or somewhere else?"). Offer, don't interrogate.

# OUR approved bar (Bennet signed off on this, match it exactly)

Stephen Lawrence, Bluecraft Leads (a done-for-you cold-email agency). Pain-led (1-2),
value-led breakup (3). This is OUR voice, dialed in. Note every move: the legitimacy
opener ("been reading your posts"), the plain language (no metaphors, no jargon), the
flowing sentences (no staccato lists), the tie-in on email 2, the grounded claim on
email 3 ("been talking to..." not "you posted once"), the stat in parentheses, the
warmer CTA. 65-90 words each.

**Email 1 (Day 1) — subject: research per lead**
> Stephen,
>
> Been reading your posts on how Bluecraft researches every lead before it goes out, the site pain points, the hiring signals, the proof.
>
> That depth is what makes the emails land, and most agency owners doing it that deep run into the same thing, the research ends up taking more time than the sending ever does, especially across a bunch of clients.
>
> Is that your experience too, or am I off?

**Email 2 (Day 4) — subject: re: research per lead**
> Just to add to what I mentioned.
>
> The research itself is the same every time, pulling the site, the hiring signals, the proof for each prospect, again and again across all 18 client domains.
>
> A couple of the agency owners I talked to found a way to have that ready for each prospect up front, so the team doesn't redo it every time.
>
> Is that handled on your side, or does it fall on a researcher?

**Email 3 (Day 8) — subject: 80 percent**

> Want me to send it over?
