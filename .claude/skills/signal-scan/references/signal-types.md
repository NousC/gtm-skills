# Signal types — the catalog signal-scan reads

This is the reference signal-scan loads in steps 3-4 to detect, classify, and score
signals. The golden rule sits above everything: **a signal only counts if it maps
to the offer loaded in step 1.** A hiring post is noise unless your offer solves
the problem that hiring implies. Always judge against *this* ICP, never a
universal template.

## 1. The six classes

### Stack — what tools they run
**Where to find:** integrations/partners page, footer logos, pricing page,
tools named in job descriptions, the Nous `tech_stack` fact.
**Implies:** what they've already bought reveals the problem they're solving and
whether a competitor owns the relationship.
**Examples:** runs a direct competitor (strong) · duct-tapes 3 point tools that
your product unifies (strong) · uses an adjacent tool in the problem space
(moderate) · obvious manual process where a tool should be (moderate).

### Hiring — roles they're posting
**Where:** /careers, /jobs, "we're hiring" posts, Nous facts.
**Implies:** where they feel operational strain and where they're investing.
**Examples:** hiring the exact role your product replaces/augments (strong) ·
multiple SDR/role posts in 30 days = scaling that motion (strong) · leadership
hire in your buyer's function (strong) · general growth hiring (moderate).

### Momentum — funding, growth, expansion
**Where:** homepage banners/news, /about, press, Nous facts.
**Implies:** budget is available and processes are about to scale (and break).
**Examples:** recent raise + no tooling for your problem (strong) · new
market/office, headcount jump (moderate-strong) · acquisition (strong, timing).

### Friction — visible pain
**Where:** public reviews (G2/Trustpilot), support/status pages, complaint-style
blog or social posts, churn/switching language.
**Implies:** a real, validated operational gap — the most actionable signal type.
**Examples:** public complaint that maps to your value prop (strong) · negative
reviews of a competitor they use (strong) · a broken-process tell on their own
site (strong).

### Intent — they're consciously working the problem
**Where (this skill):** /blog, news, resource pages, JD language. **Deep
post-based Intent (their LinkedIn posts) is the separate post-scraper skill —
not here.**
**Implies:** active awareness/evaluation of the problem space.
**Examples:** blog/JD language about the exact problem you solve (strong) ·
conference talk / resource on it (moderate-strong) · generic category content
(moderate).

### Domain — vertical/marketplace tells
**Where:** homepage, product pages, Nous facts.
**Implies:** niche-specific pain only your ICP has.
**Examples:** a vertical platform/marketplace indicator unique to your ICP
(moderate-strong) · regulatory/compliance context that creates the need
(moderate).

## 2. The scoring rubric (1-10)

Score on **exclusivity × intent** — how rare the signal is AND how strongly it
predicts the problem. Score honestly; a 4 is useful (it tells the email writer to
go softer). Don't inflate.

**8-10 — highest intent**
- direct competitor with visible friction (negative reviews, switching signals)
- active job post for a role your product replaces or augments
- public complaint / operational gap that maps directly to your value prop
- recent event (funding, acquisition, expansion) that creates immediate need

**5-7 — strong**
- adjacent/related tools that indicate the problem space (not a direct competitor)
- hiring pattern suggesting growth in the relevant area
- a tech stack that *creates* the problem you solve (duct-taping tools)
- industry/vertical match with a known pain pattern

**3-4 — moderate**
- company size/stage that typically has this problem
- general industry trend that applies
- firmographic match with no behavioural signal

**1-2 — fallback**
- basic ICP-criteria match only
- no specific behavioural or operational signal found

---

## 3. Combined patterns amplify

Signals compound — co-occurrence raises confidence beyond any single one:
- hiring + competitor usage + negative reviews → **9-10**
- funding + no existing tooling for the problem → **8-9**
- competitor usage + public customer complaints → **8-10**

When two or more classes point at the same pain, score the bundle, not the parts.

## 4. Approach mapping (runtime readout only)

For each ranked signal, the readout names the approach so the downstream
cold-email/linkedin skill is calibrated. (signal-scan does NOT write the message.)

- **Pain-led** — signal reveals a specific, acute pain. *"Noticed [signal]. Most
  in your position are dealing with [pain] — is that you?"*
- **Value-led** — you can show value first. *"Found [specific thing] for you —
  [insight]. Thought it might help."*
- **Segment fallback** — only moderate signals. Lead with the most common pain
  for the profile and ask if it resonates.

Always include a fallback approach for the rest of the segment, even when strong
signals exist.
